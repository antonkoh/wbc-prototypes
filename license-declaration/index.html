Why CC0 for structured data?

Wikibase Cloud requires that all structured data is published under CC0 or dedicated to the public domain. Here is the reasoning:

1. CC licenses were designed for creative works, not data

CC BY and CC BY-SA grant rights under copyright, but in many jurisdictions data is protected by sui generis database rights instead. CC licenses do not cover those rights cleanly.

2. Attribution requirements become unworkable at scale

If you combine 50 CC BY datasets, you may be obligated to attribute all 50 sources in every context where the combined dataset appears.

3. The Share-Alike clause in CC BY-SA creates legal risk

The boundaries of what counts as a "derivative" of a dataset are unclear. Querying or combining datasets may or may not trigger Share-Alike obligations.

4. CC BY-SA is incompatible with other open data licenses

CC BY-SA and the Open Database License (ODbL, used by OpenStreetMap) both have share-alike clauses and are mutually incompatible.

5. CC0 removes all ambiguity

CC0 waives all rights to the extent permitted by law. It works across jurisdictions whether the data is protected by copyright, database rights, or neither - and it is what Wikidata uses.
